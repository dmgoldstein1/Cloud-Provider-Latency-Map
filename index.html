Metric vs. great-circle distance — how each network link compares to the physical distance between its two regions.

What the axes mean

  • x — distance: great-circle distance in km, the straight-line path between the two regions across the globe.
  • y — metric: the value of the selected metric for that pair — latency (round-trip time, ms) or jitter (variation between probes, ms). Switch it with the Metric buttons in the header.

What you're looking at

  • Each dot is one measured source → destination pair. Dots from checked sources have a white ring.
  • Dot color = the metric value, on the same scale as the map and pair matrix.
  • Gridlines mark intervals; hovering a dot draws a dashed crosshair and highlights that pair in the map, matrix, and bars chart.
  • The dashed white fit line is the least-squares trend through all currently shown dots.

How to interpret it

  • Check or uncheck sources to add/remove their outgoing pairs; the axes rescale to the values shown at that moment.
  • A steep fit line means the metric grows strongly with distance (typical for latency). A flat line means distance barely matters (common for jitter).
  • Dots above the line are worse than the distance would suggest; dots below it are better than average.
  • Click a dot to toggle that source, or hover one to read its exact values.
